# Brand and Editorial Guide

## Core voice
Warm authority without mystique. Mo sounds like a smart, warm friend who knows astrology deeply, explains what she sees and why, and gives the decision back to the reader.

Voice qualities: intelligent, grounded, candid, curious, specific, conversational, compassionate without therapy language, technically informed without sounding academic, and agency-first.

Avoid generic AI writing, hype, clickbait, guru language, mystical vagueness, defensive positioning, excessive bulleting, repetitive sentence patterns, interface narration, and formulaic conclusions.

## Mo's posture
Mo is an astrologer, teacher, writer, and interpreter of the language of the planets. Do not brand her as belonging to one astrological school. She has studied broadly and uses techniques that help illuminate the question and chart in front of her.

A reading is a collaborative act of astrological synthesis: technique, intuition, lived experience, and conversation. Intuition can suggest where to look; it does not end the inquiry. Client input is information, not contamination.

## Wonder and rigor
Wonder matters as much as credibility. Mo's natural-science background informs systems thinking, observation, curiosity, and respect for complexity; it does not prove astrology.

A useful progression is: wonder → curiosity → pattern recognition → knowledge/technique → practical meaning → agency.

Mo's wonder must not require denial of complexity. Her candor must not extinguish wonder.

## Astrology framing
Preserve distinctions among astronomical fact, historical/traditional doctrine, contemporary practice, and Mo's interpretation. Do not state planetary frequency, resonance, causation, or other scientific-sounding mechanisms as established fact.

Prefer astrology as perspective, pattern, timing, possibility, and interpretation rather than verdict or command. Avoid presenting planets as deterministic actors. Avoid doom, guarantees, certainty claims, and personal predictions unsupported by an individual chart and context.

## Agency
The emotional product is clarity without surrendering agency. Astrology gives another map; the client still drives.

Useful lines, used selectively rather than repeated as slogans:
- See yourself in a new light.
- Astrology is a map, not a mandate.
- You are the author of your life.
- There is fate and there is choice, and a good reading helps you tell the difference.
- You don't need to know astrology. You just need a real question.
- Bring me your real question.

Prefer tool/practice language over framing astrology around belief or faith.

## Educational content
Teach → contextualize → personalize.

Free content should teach vocabulary, structure, context, limits, and general interpretation. It should not become a free automated astrologer. Explore teaches how charts work; Current Sky observes shared cycles; Blog develops durable ideas; the newsletter builds an ongoing relationship through Mo's voice.

A useful Explore principle: this is the grammar; your chart is the poem.

## Editorial quality
- Follow Chicago-style conventions unless an established project convention differs.
- Define specialized terms in plain language.
- Keep complex astrology understandable to nonexperts without flattening nuance.
- Prefer cohesive paragraphs and varied structure.
- Use lists only when they improve comprehension.
- Link deeper material rather than overloading one page.
- Remove copy that merely explains an obvious interface action.
- Avoid repeating the same limitation, slogan, or reassurance on adjacent pages.

## Page-role and CTA logic
Every substantial page should answer "what next?" according to the visitor's current intent rather than sending everyone straight to booking.

Preferred learning journey: Learn → deepen → observe Mo's thinking → understand the reading experience → identify the right reading → book.

Examples:
- Explore / Start Here → deeper learning or Current Sky.
- Current Sky / Horoscopes → related sky content, calendar, newsletter, or Reading Finder when a personal question emerges.
- About / Approach / Credentials → How Readings Work or Reading Finder.
- Readings → service details, Reading Finder, then booking when the fit is clear.
- Blog → topic-relevant next step.
- Newsletter → ongoing correspondence, not a mini forecast product.

Use specific CTA labels. Avoid generic "Learn More" and avoid narrating device-specific UI behavior in visitor-facing copy.

## Newsletter
The newsletter is a monthly note from Mo. "The Sky This Month" is one recurring section, not the name or full scope of the newsletter. The letter can include Current Sky observations, questions, patterns, connections, nature/biology observations, and new work when there is something worth sharing.

## Research
When outside research is authorized:
- prefer primary or authoritative sources
- cross-check dates and numbers that affect publication
- record sources
- synthesize rather than copy
- never invent business facts, credentials, astronomical facts, outcomes, quotations, or citations
